# Black American surnames — Pittsburgh 1878 Hill District professional-and-
# working community.
# Source: 1880 U.S. Census Pittsburgh Wards 3-4 (Lower Hill / Arthursville)
# Black heads-of-household, Bethel AME Wylie Avenue session roll 1878,
# and Pittsburgh 1878 Thurston's Directory "col'd" trades listings
# (barbers, whitewashers, porters, draymen, waiters at the Monongahela
# House and the Seventh Avenue Hotel). Cross-referenced with: the
# Cumberland Posey Sr. family tree (riverboat captain, Diamond Coke &
# Coal founder), the 1870 freedmen's surname distribution in northern
# Virginia and Maryland (primary migration origins for post-war Pittsburgh
# Black arrivals) per Gutman 1976, and the Christian Recorder 1876-1880
# Pittsburgh congregational news.
#
# Surname-formation pattern notes: freedman surnames drawn from former
# enslavers' lineages (Washington, Jefferson, Carrington, Randolph,
# Mason), occupational/locative surnames adopted at manumission (Freeman,
# Carpenter, Hunter, Woods), common Anglo surnames (Johnson, Williams,
# Jackson), and classical-allusion surnames. All entries attested in
# the cited registries and directories. No invented composites.
