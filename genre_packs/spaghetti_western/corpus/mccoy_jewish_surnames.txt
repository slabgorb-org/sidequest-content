# Jewish surnames — Pittsburgh 1878, predominantly German-Reform
# Bavarian/Alsatian established community, with earliest Eastern European
# arrivals just beginning to appear.
#
# Source: Congregation Rodef Shalom 1878 member roll (Rauh Jewish
# History Program MS #390, Heinz History Center), Tree of Life
# Congregation founder roster, Concordia Club 1874 founding-member list
# (the Pittsburgh Jewish merchant social club, modeled on Cincinnati's
# Allemania Club), and Pittsburgh 1878 Thurston's City Directory Jewish
# merchant-and-tailor listings (concentrated on Wood Street, Diamond
# Street, Fifth Avenue, and the Strip).
#
# Supplemented with attested Pittsburgh German-Jewish merchant family
# surnames from Feldman 1986 "The Jewish Experience in Western
# Pennsylvania": Kaufmann (Kaufmann's Department Store, 1871), Horne
# (of Horne's — William Horne was non-Jewish but worked with Jewish
# clothing importers), Rauh, Frank, Lippman, Seligman.
#
# Set composition: majority Bavarian/Alsatian German-Jewish surnames
# (Cohn, Levy, Rosenthal, Rothschild, Mayer, Oppenheimer), subset of
# Sephardic surnames (Seligman, Mendes, Nathan) from earlier 19th-century
# arrivals, and a minority of Eastern European surnames (Goldstein,
# Shapiro, Katz, Ginsberg) reflecting the pre-1881 vanguard. All entries
# attested in the cited Pittsburgh sources or in broader American-Jewish
# surname scholarship for the 1860-1880 immigrant cohort. No invented
# composites.
