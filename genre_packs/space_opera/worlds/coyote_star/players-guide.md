# Coyote Star — Player Briefing

## The System, in One Breath

A single star at the end of a dead-end jump lane. One way in, one way out, and they're the same door — **Grand Gate**, six and a half AU out from the sun. Whoever holds the gate holds the system. The Hegemony technically holds it. *Technically* is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

You're somewhere in here right now, on a freighter called the **Kestrel**, and the morning — such as morning means on a ship that has no morning — is yours.

## What's Out There

**Far Landing.** The habitable inner world. Dry, windy, breathable, ugly. Home of **Vaskov Centrum**, the Hegemonic governor's seat. Most settlers live here or grew up here. Dust roads, mining towns, water cooperatives, and an aging patrol cruiser parked at *Tethys Watch* overhead.

**Deep Root.** The second world's mountain range. The **Tsveri** live here — the native species. Tall, slow, silicon-skinned, vibrating in registers your chest can hear before your ears do. They claim the mountain. They do not negotiate. The miners keep finding rare-earths under sacred ground anyway. Nobody good will come of that.

**The Broken Drift.** The asteroid belt. Where most of the system's ore comes from, and where most of the system's quiet cargo also gets moved. The Free Miners' Compact runs it on paper. The Broken Drift Runners run it when no one's looking.

**Mendes' Post.** The Compact's welded-together capital — three barge hulls, two cargo cans, an aftermarket spin module that creaks at watch-change. Galley, bar, dock. **Dura Mendes** runs Compact business out of the back office. If you're inbound from the Gate, you coast here first to ask what's changed.

**Turning Hub.** Three voidborn station hulls jumped through the Gate forty years ago and welded into a freeport at the gas giant. Clan **Moana-Teru** runs it. Customs writ does *not* run here. They control most of the fuel in the system and pretend they don't.

**Red Prospect.** The gas giant. Hydrogen scoops, atmospheric tankers, outer moons that keep their own quiet councils.

**The Last Drift.** The thin halo past the gas giant. Charted in literal pencil. Recently, prospectors have been reporting energy readings from bodies that shouldn't be generating energy. Nobody's filed a claim. Nobody's filed a claim *against* it either.

## Beyond the Gate

Coyote Star is a dead end. To get anywhere else, you go back through **Grand Gate** — and where you go from there depends on which jump lane you take next. Almost no one in the system has done it in years.

**The Hegemonic Core.** The empire's seat. A cluster of older systems linked by half-a-dozen jump points apiece, where charcoal uniforms come from the registry and aren't held together with patches. Slavic-led, multi-world, the bureaucracy written down in three different alphabets. Most of what gets called "the Hegemony" out here is whatever survived the trip through the Gate.

**Old Earth.** Far back, far through the lanes — the mother world. You won't see it. Most spacers won't. But Earth *culture* is everywhere out here, at the resolution of a half-remembered movie. Everyone has a vague idea what a "western" is — the dust, the gun, the lone rider — even if they couldn't tell you the difference between Texas and Tombstone. Everyone knows what a samurai looks like, what the Mafia is, what a pharaoh was. Push past that surface and you're an *Earth nerd* — the kind of person who can name which side won the Civil War, list three Egyptian dynasties in order, and tell you whether the Vikings really wore horned helmets. Earth nerds are a known subculture. They tend to find each other; other people find them tiresome at parties.

Most of what you see in Coyote Star is Earth culture run through a long game of telephone. The Free Miners think of themselves as a frontier — they got that word from somewhere. The voidborn name their ships after oceans. The Hegemony's uniforms are stitched from ideas about empire that started on a peninsula nobody alive has seen. The world you live in is a remix.

**The colonised worlds.** Dozens of them between Earth and here. Names you might recognise if your grandfather did. Worlds the Hegemony absorbed, worlds that joined, worlds the maps don't show because the lanes that reach them are bad. Most Hegemonic surnames carry the trace of one — the second-place homeworld a family picked up two generations back, kept like a maiden name.

**Where the voidborn come from.** Clan Moana-Teru remembers a homeworld with oceans. They came through the Gate forty years ago in three station hulls, which means they came from somewhere they could *build* three station hulls, which means they came from a freeport or a shipyard system — ask which one and you'll get a different answer from every elder who'll talk to you. The ocean part is the part that's never in dispute.

**The Tsveri were here first.** They didn't come through the Gate. They were on Deep Root before any human charted the lane. What they know about the worlds beyond the system is whatever they decide to share, which, in living memory, is nothing.

Coyote Star is the end of the road. The road keeps going behind you. Out there are governments, wars, harvests, holidays, prayers, debts, fashions, and things you don't have to think about anymore. A lot of you are here because of that.

## The Kestrel

Voidborn freighter. Your home for this campaign. Cockpit, engineering, galley, deck-three corridor. A captain, an engineer, a doc, a cook. She runs the **Mendes Coast** on autopilot — fourteen hours from Far Landing out to Mendes' Post, give or take a Hegemonic delay. She has a hum. The voidborn call it *the breath of a ship that knows where she's going*.

You're aboard. Whatever shift it is — yours.

## What You'll Be Asked at the Table

Five questions. There's no wrong answer; there's freeform on every one of them if none of these fit. Think on them. Pick the one that sounds like the shape of the year you've been having.

**1. How did you end up out here?**
- *I came through the Gate.* Outsystem-arrived, with a duffel and a story.
- *Far Landing raised me.* Dust-born. The only place you've ever been.
- *Turning Hub was the whole world.* Voidborn corridors, dock schedules before books.
- *I lived near the Deep Root.* Hill-country adjacent to the Tsveri. You learned to listen.

**2. Pronouns?** she/her, he/him, they/them, or your own.

**3. What did you do out here?**
- *I held the bridge.* Captain of your own boat — small hauler, salvage tug, prospector skiff, doesn't matter what it was, you ran it. The crew called you skipper. Some still do.
- *I worked the rig.* Compact Hand. Engineer.
- *I flew the belt.* Survey Runner. Pilot.
- *I ran the drift.* Drift Runner. Smuggler.
- *I traded in words.* Fixer / broker. Diplomat.

**4. What cut you loose?**
- *Someone went into the Drift.* A name you don't say anymore.
- *A debt followed you through the Gate.* Not money. A promise.
- *Your place got dissolved.* Claim, unit, corridor, settlement — whatever held you is gone.
- *You saw something out past the gas giant.* And you went back. Twice.

**5. When things go sideways, what do you bring?**
- *Steady hands.* Calm under pressure.
- *A sharp mouth.* Velocity, not charm.
- *Loyalty.* Once you're in, you're in. Non-negotiable.
- *The wrong kind of luck.* Things happen around you. Everyone wants you on their crew anyway.

## What to Think About Before You Show Up

- **What's your handle?** If you ran the Drift, you have one. Even if you didn't, you might.
- **Who's the name you don't say?** Crew, family, a captain, a sibling who took the Pencil Arc and didn't come back. Bring one.
- **What did you carry through the Gate?** Not gear — the *thing*. The reason you can't quite look at the inbound jump traffic without checking it.
- **What do you owe the Kestrel?** She's been good to you. Long enough that you fix her on muscle memory. Why are you still aboard?

Coyote Star is wide. The crew is the crew. Bring questions, bring trouble, bring something worth losing.
