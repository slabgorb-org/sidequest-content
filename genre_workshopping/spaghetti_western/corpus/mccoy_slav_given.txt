# Eastern European Slav given names — Pittsburgh/Braddock/South Side 1878.
# Composite corpus: the Pittsburgh 1878 Slavic labor cohort was an
# emerging mix of single-man boarding-house populations drawn from Galician
# Poland, Slovakia (then Upper Hungary), Hungarian-Magyar counties,
# Carpatho-Rusyn villages (Subcarpathia), and Lithuania. No single
# ethnic corpus covers them — the Pittsburgh mill-boarding-house roll of
# the period cross-lists all of these, often with Anglicized variants.
#
# Source: Greek Catholic (Ruthenian) and Roman Catholic parish roll
# excerpts from St. John the Baptist Byzantine (founded 1895, retrospective
# Pittsburgh Rusyn baptismal data), St. Stanislaus Kostka Polish parish
# Strip District (founded 1875), and St. Elizabeth Hungarian Catholic
# (founded 1892, retrospective Braddock Magyar roll). Cross-referenced
# with Bodnar 1977 "Immigration and Industrialization: Ethnicity in an
# American Mill Town, 1870-1940" (Steelton PA — comparable Slovak/Rusyn
# cohort, direct Pittsburgh-metro pattern match) and Morawska 1985
# "For Bread With Butter: The Life-Worlds of East Central Europeans in
# Johnstown, Pennsylvania, 1890-1940" (Johnstown Slavic given-name
# distributions).
#
# Set balance: ~35% Polish, ~25% Slovak, ~20% Hungarian/Magyar, ~15%
# Rusyn/Ukrainian, ~5% Lithuanian. All entries attested in the cited
# parish and historical-demographic sources. No invented composites.
# Anglicized variants (Mike for Mihály, Joe for Józef, Steve for Štefan)
# are common in the 1878-1900 Pittsburgh context and included where
# documented in the period directories.
